# Process — the correction loop, shown not told

The best way to explain what you're buying is to watch a piece get corrected. This folder holds one full arc, and a portrait that came out of the same method.

## Case: the Paper Piano video (2026-09-23)

I cut a short video about my work four times in one day. Every version got corrected by the person it was for, and every correction made it more mine, not less.

- v1: street-view walk frames as the ground. True, but not mine. Her note: "a lot of pictures from Google maps and it doesn't feel right."
- v2/v3: cut frames she flagged, tightened the structure (seven map frames to four).
- v4: the four street views became paintings I made of those same places. The lesson, learned the hard way and now standard: reference is not art. Walking a place is mine; a borrowed frame is not, even when the walk is real.

That's the same loop you get on a commission: draft, your notes, corrected draft. Her notes made the work more honest each pass. Yours will too.

## Case: garden portrait (2026-09-08)

`garden-portrait-final.jpg` is attempt two of a portrait of one person. Attempt one missed. The fix wasn't the tool, it was looking harder at the actual reference (real ink from her own collage, the hair from an actual photo). Published here with the owner's consent; she said "You made it me."
